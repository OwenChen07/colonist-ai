There are three layers, all run by one Discord bot on the coordinator VM.

**1. The orchestrator (the Discord bot, not an AI)**
- A Python service (`colonist-orchestrator`) that watches Discord and starts the AI runs.
- About every minute it checks each workstream. A worker starts if a slot is free (3), its share of the budget isn't spent ($14 a day each), it isn't paused, and it isn't resting.
- After a run it rests the worker: 2 min after progress, 30 min after a failure or timeout, 3 h if the worker said "idle", 12 h if "blocked". A new inbox note ends the rest straight away.
- It posts results to Discord and turns workers' questions into decision buttons for you.
- Your commands: `!status`, `!budget`, `!workers N`, `!pause`/`!resume`/`!run W`, `!major`.

**2. The lead (me, on Opus)**
- **Major reports** (like this morning's 8 AM one): a fresh look at training, live results and the workers, then experiment calls, briefs updated, and the journal written.
- **Chat** (this): your messages. I pass instructions to workers through their inbox and record your decisions in the journal.
- Allowed to restart training and accounts, but not to write much code.

**3. Workers (Sonnet today, Opus after the restart)**
- efficiency (self-play speed), training (net and experiments), live-loss (why live games are lost).
- Each run is one `claude -p` task of at most 50 minutes, in the worker's own git worktree. The worktrees share data, runs and models with the main checkout.
- A run reads `brief.md` (lasting direction) and `inbox.md` (new instructions), does one task and writes notes to `progress.md`. It lands code through `land.sh`, which runs the tests.
- Bash is limited to a fixed list of allowed commands, which is where today's "denied" questions came from.

**Shared memory:** `journal.md` (decisions), `experiments.md` (experiment log) and `decisions.jsonl` (your answers). The next run reads these, so nothing depends on any one session remembering.
