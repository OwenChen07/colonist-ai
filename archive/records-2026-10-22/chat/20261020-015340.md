Yes, in narrow forms, and no as a general direction. The evidence from our own ledger and from OpenHex points both ways.

**Where rules have not helped us:** E17 bolted an endgame rule filter onto the search's root moves and scored 51.5% ± 5.8% over 200 games, noise, so it was dropped. Generic "common sense" rules tend to override the search exactly where the search was already right.

**Where rules clearly carry value:** on OpenHex the rule-based bot rehex sits at 1573, above our 1512, with no net at all, though we beat it 11-5 head to head. That says hand-written Catan knowledge gets you a long way, and a net can miss simple things such as the road join from tonight.

**The three forms I would try, cheapest first:**
1. **Rules as input features.** Compute facts the net struggles to infer and hand them in: the longest-road length after each legal road, whether a build secures or breaks Longest Road, cards needed for the next build, whether the opponent can win next turn. The net stays in charge; it just stops having to derive geometry. This is the fix I proposed for the road join and it generalises.
2. **Forced-move checks at the root.** If a move wins now, play it. If the opponent wins next turn unless blocked, only consider blocks. Exact, cheap, never wrong, and the search sometimes misses them under the 5-second cap.
3. **Rules as opponents in self-play.** A rule-based road-racer or city-builder in the opponent pool gives the net the plans people use. That is the league idea research is scoping.

What I would not do is let rules pick moves over the search in ordinary positions; E17 is the warning, and every such rule needs a 300-game A/B.

Forms 1 and 2 fit in the clock and training work already queued, so I will have research include them in its league and feature plan rather than open a separate experiment.
