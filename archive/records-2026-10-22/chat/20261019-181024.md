The gate is the "progress" gate set by E38 on Oct 18, since round 96. Here is what a new net must pass each round:

**The match.** The new net plays the current best, round 73, on paired boards at 200 simulations a move:
- 4,000 games. It passes at 51% or better.
- A near miss between 50% and 52% gets 4,000 more games on fresh boards, and is judged on all 8,000. At 8,000 games the margin is about ±1.1%, so a real 1.5% gain can show.
- Below 50% after 4,000 it is kept, and a match that cannot reach the bar after the first 1,000 games is stopped early.

**Three guardrails, checked only after a pass.** The net is promoted only if none fails:
1. **Anchor:** its score against a fixed old net, round 13, over 2,000 games, must not drop more than 2 points below the best net's score against the same anchor. This catches a slow slide that a match against last round's net cannot see.
2. **Benchmark:** its score against the fixed AlphaBeta reference must not be clearly below the best's.
3. **Live value error:** its error on the held-out human games must not rise by more than 0.01.

**Why it looks this way.** The earlier confidence gate needed about 52.2% over 2,000 games, which a real 1% gain can never reach, so rounds 74 to 95 all stayed at 47 to 50.6% with no information. The progress gate trades a lower bar for longer matches and leans on the guardrails against a false promotion. Since then no round has passed either: rounds 111 to 121 pooled at 49.9% over 68,000 games, and today's rounds sit around 49.7%.

What a promotion would mean: a net that is better than round 73 by at least a point in self-play and no worse on the three guardrails. What it would not mean: a live gain, which has to be measured over 100+ ranked games afterwards, since round 73 and round 66 play the same live despite the gate's verdict.
