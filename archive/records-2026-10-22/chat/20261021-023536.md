Mostly no. The opening placements use the same search and the same net as every other move. There is no opening book and no separate opening model. Two things do differ:

- **More time and more simulations.** Colonist gives the first settlement 60 seconds, so the bot spends up to 20 seconds and about 20,000 simulations on it, against 5 seconds and about 4,800 for a normal turn decision. The opening road gets 5 seconds and about 4,000. The roll gets 1 second and 800.
- **In training only, extra randomness.** Self-play adds noise to the first settlement choice so the training games cover many openings. That noise is off in live play, where the bot simply takes the best-scoring spot.

Everything else is identical: the net's prior over spots, the search's lookahead, the value estimate, and the hidden-card handling.

One caveat worth knowing. The net's view of an opening spot comes mostly from self-play against itself, and the search looks only a few turns ahead at that stage. So the bot's placements are good by its own standards but not benchmarked against what strong players do. Live-play's opening study, comparing our first two placements with the 2300+ players' choices in the human dataset, is still queued behind the account work and needs a quiet machine. It is the natural next step if you want the opening looked at on its own.
