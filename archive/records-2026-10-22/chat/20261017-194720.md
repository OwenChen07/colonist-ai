**Kill:** done as a restricted command rather than a blanket `kill`. A small script, `kill_landing.sh`, that can only stop landing and test processes (`land.sh`, its lock waiters, its `pytest`), and refuses anything else, so neither I nor a worker can take down the training loop, the bots or the orchestrator by mistake. Allowed for both the lead and the workers.

**Percent of usage: yes, with one caveat.** Claude Code's own `/usage` screen gets its numbers from an Anthropic endpoint, using the login already on the coordinator. It returns the 5-hour and weekly utilization as percentages, plus when each resets. That's exactly what the plan enforces, so it's a better gate than the dollar estimates. The caveat: the endpoint is undocumented, so it could change without notice. The design handles that:
- Workers don't start when the 5-hour window is above 85% or the week above 90% (both adjustable), leaving headroom for reports and chat.
- `!budget` and the status posts show "5 h: 42%, week: 61%" next to the dollars.
- If the endpoint fails, the dollar budget stays as the fallback.
- The worker will ask you to compare its numbers with what `/usage` shows before we rely on it.

The status-post part needs one more orchestrator restart once it lands; the worker will say so in its post.
