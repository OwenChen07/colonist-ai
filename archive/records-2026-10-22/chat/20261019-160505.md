Only one thing differs. Petra4620 has run a human opponent model since Oct 12: inside the search, the opponent's replies are predicted half by our own net and half by a model trained on how people actually play (experiment E2). The other three accounts use the same net, the same budget and the same settings, and predict the opponent purely from our own net. A side effect is that Petra gets about 15% fewer simulations per move, since the second model costs CPU on the same VM.

Does that explain the lead? Probably not, or not much:
- E2 was judged on 483 games: Petra 57.6% with the model, the control accounts 57.4% over the same period. No difference, so it was left on Petra and not rolled out.
- Since round 73 went live, Petra is 61-32 (66%) against 63%, 55% and 52% on the others. Each number carries about ±10%, so Petra against the weakest is a 14-point gap with a 14-point margin. Suggestive, not proven.
- The confound runs the other way: Petra is the highest-rated account, so it meets stronger opponents and should win less, not more. If the gap is real, the model may help only against stronger players, which E2's early period at 1900-2200 could not see.

Plain noise between accounts is the other explanation; live-loss has seen an account swing 10 points over a few days on its run of opponents.

The clean test is cheap: put the opponent model on Dale7112, the weakest account, and compare the two accounts with it against the two without over 300 games each, split by opponent rating. That is the live-play workstream's kind of job, so I have noted it in the journal as its second experiment after the clock-use table, for the 3 PM run to hand over.
