Here is my reading, with what our own experiments say for each cause and how other systems handle it.

**1. The teacher is barely ahead of the student.** Our loop is AlphaZero-style: the net learns to copy what the search chooses. That only works while the search is clearly better than the raw net. Research measured this morning that at 400 simulations the search beats the net only 59%, and two searches of the same position disagree 10% of the time. So each round teaches the net almost nothing new, and noise. Catan makes this worse than chess or Go: dice and hidden cards cap how much a search can see ahead. Others: KataGo and Leela raise the search quality per position and add richer targets; MuZero re-searches old positions with the newest net. Ours: E51 (800 simulations) and E59 (less noisy targets) are exactly this, verdicts tonight.

**2. Self-play only ever meets itself.** The net is trained against its own style, so plans it never uses, such as the road race, never appear in its data, and it is weak against them live. AlphaStar solved this with a league: a pool of past versions plus "exploiter" agents trained only to beat the main agent. Poker and Diplomacy work keeps a human-like policy in the mix. Ours: the people-model seat in 15% of games is a small version. I have asked research to scope a proper league with an exploiter net for the 9 PM report.

**3. The value target is noisy.** A game's result is a coin flip on the dice as much as on the position, so the net learns slowly from outcomes. KataGo uses score and ownership targets, not just win or loss. Ours: the late-game calibration experiments (E35, E49) touched this; a score-margin target is untried.

**4. Self-play strength is not the goal.** Round 73 beat round 66 in the gate and plays identically against people. We optimise one target and measure another. Diplomacy's Cicero fixed the same gap by regularising toward human play. Ours: today's exact-hand human data (E56) and the people-model account (E57) are the first real steps.

What is ruled out by evidence: net size (E40), epochs (E31, E46), window (E37), learning rate and averaging (E47, E48).

My ranking of the levers: 2 and 4 first, since they change what the net learns rather than how hard it learns it, then 1, then 3.
