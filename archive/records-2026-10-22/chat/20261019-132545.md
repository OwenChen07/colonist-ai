Yes, and two methods fit the evidence. Research is now scoping both with costs for the 3 PM report; nothing in production changes until then.

**What we run now** is already a kind of RL: AlphaZero-style self-play, where the search's choices teach the net. It sits at a fixed point. Research measured this morning that search at 400 simulations beats the raw net only 59%, 800 beats 400 by 54%, and two searches of the same position disagree 10% of the time. The teacher is barely ahead of the student and noisy, so every recipe gives round 73 back.

**Two different methods:**

1. **Policy-gradient RL without search (PPO-style).** Train the net directly on game outcomes from fast, searchless games against a pool of past nets. Thousands of games per hour per core instead of about 150, so 20-50x the data, and E11b said the net is data-limited. Supporting evidence: on OpenHex the top bot is a plain net with no search, beating the searching versions, and E18 found search worth only 1-2 points here. Risk: dice make the outcome signal noisy, so it needs many games and a good value baseline. Rough cost: two days of research's time on the PC's GPU plus a fleet slice.

2. **Train against people-like opponents, not only itself.** Build a behaviour-cloned "human" policy from our captures and ranked games, then fine-tune the net against a mix of itself and that model. This aims straight at the live gap: the bot is calibrated against itself (late value error 0.34) but not against people (0.43), and loses to specific human plans. Risk: our human data is thin, and E14 and E23, which only added human games to the training set, showed nothing live. Rough cost: three days.
