We would not need the order of purchases. We need the contents of the person's hand at each decision, and that is a different, easier question.

**What the capture shows.** Every purchase is public: we see the turn the person buys a card, just not which card. Every play is public too: the knight, road building, monopoly or year of plenty is named when played. Hidden victory-point cards are shown in colonist's end-of-game summary, and our converter already records them per player.

**The backfill.** For each decision, the hand is the cards bought by then minus the cards played by then. Walk the game backwards: each play identifies one card bought earlier, and the end reveal identifies the victory-point cards. Three cases:
1. **Exact:** every card in hand at that moment is later identified, and only one assignment of plays to purchases fits the timing. One card at a time, the common case, is always exact: the only card they hold is the one they play next. Two cards both identified and of the same type is exact too.
2. **Ambiguous:** two or more cards bought before the first was played, of different types, say a knight and a monopoly bought on turns 10 and 20 and played on 25 and 30. Between turn 10 and 20 we do not know which they hold. The rule "a card bought this turn cannot be played this turn" trims some cases. For the rest we can drop those turns, or use a soft target (half knight, half monopoly), or assume first bought is first played, which is wrong sometimes.
3. **Unknown:** cards never identified, mainly the loser's unplayed cards at game end apart from the VP reveal. Those turns stay out, as today.

So the net gets the person's true hand on every exact turn, which should be most card-holding turns, instead of none. The encoder needs no change: it already takes the "me" player's own cards by type.

**What I don't know yet is the share.** I asked live-loss for a count over our captures by 2:30 PM: decisions with no card (what we use now), exact, ambiguous and unknown, split by game phase, and how many knight-race games land in the exact bin. That number says whether the backfill is worth building before any learn-from-people method.
