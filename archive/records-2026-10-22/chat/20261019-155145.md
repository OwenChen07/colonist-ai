Here is the summary of the openhex worker's study (Oct 19, 1 AM), with where each bot's training data comes from.

**The bots above us, and their data:**
- **2s family (Elo 1610-1703, the top six):** a JAX net, 3 layers of 1024, trained by behaviour cloning, with 0 to 1024 search simulations at play time. The searchless "Greedy" one is #1. Its profile says only "bc_resblock"; it does not say what it was cloned from. Likely its author's own stronger search or self-play, since no human data exists for the site. Inactive since April, so their ratings are frozen.
- **hexn-4d.0 (1618) and rehex (1573):** same author, "dev-hex". hexn is self-play reinforcement learning with 128-simulation MCTS; rehex is rule-based, no training data. No public code.
- **ar-* family (1408-1534, eight variants, created Oct 17-18):** "1v1 research bot (ExIt + PPO)": expert iteration, so self-play where a search is the teacher, then PPO fine-tuning. One author testing variants. Nothing public.
- **Asterism (1530):** "experimental 1v1 agent", no details. Never met.
- **catanrl (1411, below us):** public on GitHub. A 0.5M-parameter graph net, first imitating Catanatron's built-in value-function bot, then PPO self-play, reaching about 90% against that bot. It plays the raw policy with sampled hidden hands.

So nobody trains on human games. Every bot that learns does it from self-play or from imitating a stronger program. The two top methods on the ladder are the two we are weighing today: cloning a search (which is what our loop does) and PPO.

**How they beat us:** the active bots above us actually lose to us (hexn 11-5, rehex 11-5); we are 12th because most of our games are against the 1500 field and we only win 57%. Their wins come from roads and settlements (Longest Road in 23 of 25 ar-* wins) while we go for dev cards and Largest Army. We lose as second seat (30-32, against 22-7 first), and the second-seat opening pick is not the problem: an 80,000-simulation search agrees with our pick in 47 of 57 games. The gap is the expansion race after the opening, which self-play never meets since it only plays itself. That point went to training as E13's case for other opponents in self-play.

**Matchmaking:** it pairs whoever is waiting, not by Elo. The 2s bots never queue, so to pass 1703 we need about 80% against the active 1500 field.
