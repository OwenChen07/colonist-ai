Yes, partly. It's the blocker in two places, and the fixes differ.

**1. Live play (the most direct lever).** The bot's strength on colonist.io comes from search: about 4800 simulations per move in 5 s. In AlphaZero-style bots, doubling search is usually worth a steady Elo gain. bot3/bot4 share one VM and bot5/bot7 another, so each account gets half a machine. If those VMs are saturated, bigger VMs (or one bot per VM) buy strength immediately, at a few dollars a day, with no training risk. I'd test it first: a 400-game self-play match of 4800 vs 9600 sims, and CPU readings on the bot VMs.

**2. The training loop.** A round is 1.7-4.7 h, so 5-ish rounds a day. Self-play is 67 min, training 14 min, but the promotion match (2000-4000 games, 75-140 min) and guardrails are the long part, and the match shares the same CPUs with the next round's self-play. Also E11b's verdict was that the net is short of data, not size. Both point the same way: more CPU. The fleet is 7 machines plus the PC; ten 4-core spot VMs (roughly $10 a day) would about double it, giving 10,000 games a round or faster matches, your choice.

**Not a blocker:** the GPU. Training is 14 min a round. Offline experiments (E16) queue behind it but that's fine.

**What compute won't fix:** round 67 just failed (48.6%), and promotions give +7-14 self-play Elo each, which comes out smaller live. At +26 a day needed, we also need the bigger ideas (E14, E16) to land, not just a faster loop.
