**Round speed:** about 21 minutes for a normal round, now that the matches are fast. Training takes 4.5 minutes, the 2,000-game match about 16. The 5,000 self-play games for the next round run during the match, so they add nothing. A near miss extends the match to 4,000 games and the round to about 45 minutes (round 85 today). Rounds 86, 87 and 88 each took 21 minutes.

**Longer rounds and promotion:** not by themselves, in my view. Promotion needs a real gain, and the data says the nets aren't gaining: rounds 74 to 88 all sit at 47 to 51% against round 73, and the validation loss hasn't moved for 15 rounds. Doubling the games per round doubles the fresh data, but the net already isn't extracting more from the data it has (the extra epochs did nothing, which is why we cut to one). It would also halve the number of tries per day. The levers I'd bet on are better training targets, which E23 (more weight on people's games) and E16 (blended value target) test, not more of the same self-play. I'll hold the round size until those verdicts.

**When a round fails:** nothing starts from scratch.
- The failed round's games still go into the training window (the last 6 rounds), so the data is kept.
- The next round trains from the failed round's net if it scored at least 48% against the best net. Round 84 scored 47%, so round 85 trained from the best net (round 73) instead. That guard stops a bad net dragging the next rounds down.
- Self-play for the next round uses the newest net the same way, with a one-round lag because it starts early.
- The live bots only switch on a promotion, so a failed round never touches them.
