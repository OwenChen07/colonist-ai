The game is flagged and handed to live-loss for the position-level analysis. Here is the answer on teaching.

**Why the bot can miss a join.** The net sees each road as an edge on the board and knows each player's current longest-road length, but it has to work out for itself that one specific edge would merge two segments into a road two or three longer. That is a multi-step geometric fact, and a graph net with three message-passing rounds often does not carry it. The search can rescue it only if the Longest Road swing shows up within its horizon; a join that pays off one build later is easy to miss at 2,400 simulations when the value head does not already know it. The road race is our known weak spot: on OpenHex the opponent took Longest Road in 19 of 21 second-seat losses, and people who beat the bot build fewer dead roads.

**Three ways to teach it, cheapest first:**
1. **An input feature per legal road:** the longest-road length the player would have after building it, computed by the engine. The net then reads the join directly instead of inferring it. Small change, one training run to test. My recommendation if the pattern is common.
2. **A targeted search budget:** we already have a tool that gives contested Longest Road positions more search; it was piloted, never deployed. It costs time per move, not training.
3. **More road-racing opponents in self-play:** the league and exploiter work research is scoping, so the net sees the race often enough to learn it from outcomes.

**What live-loss does with the flagged game:** finds the capture, replays the turn, runs the search on the join against the move played at the live budget and at 10,000 simulations, and says whether the search saw the join and rejected it or never looked. Then it counts, over the last 1,000 games, how often the bot passed up a joining road that would have taken or secured Longest Road. That count decides between the three fixes.
