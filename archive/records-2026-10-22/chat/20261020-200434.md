E54 is research's test of a different way to train the net: learning straight from who wins the game, without the search in the loop.

**How we train normally.** The bot plays itself with search (400 simulations a move), and the net is trained to copy what the search chose. That is the AlphaZero loop. Its weakness, measured in E51: the search is barely better than the raw net (59% head to head) and noisy, so the teacher is only a little ahead of the student. That is one explanation for the 77-round drought.

**What E54 does instead.** The raw net, no search, plays thousands of games against a pool of earlier nets. Moves that led to wins get more likely, moves that led to losses less likely (policy gradient, the PPO method), with a penalty for drifting too far from round 73 so the result still works as the prior inside our search. Because there is no search, a game costs 0.19 seconds instead of about 11: roughly 60 times the games per core. One iteration is 20,000 games on the PC's GPU and a 16-core share of the fleet.

**Why it matters.** If it works, we get a stronger net to put inside the same search, and a training loop not stuck at the self-play fixed point. The risk is a net that is better raw but overconfident, which makes search worse. The drift penalty and the searched gate guard against that.

**Where it is.** Iteration 30 of 30 finishes about now. Results so far: at iteration 20 it beats round 73 raw 54.7%; at iteration 10, with search on both sides, 53.8%. Next is the one decisive check: the best iteration against round 152 at 200 simulations a move, 2,000 games on the fleet, plus its value error on held-out human games. At 52% or better with no worse human error, it becomes a candidate seed for production and I decide the trial. Below that, E54 closes and E60 (the next idea on the list) starts.
