Yes, a VM can replace it, and the loop would barely slow down. @owner, your call on the spend.

**What the PC does now:** it trains the net each round. One pass over 3.8 million positions takes about 3 minutes on its GPU, and the whole training step about 4.5 minutes of a 25-minute round. It also plays about 3% of self-play (small) and hosts the offline experiment runs like E23.

**The net is tiny** (102k parameters), so the training time is mostly data loading, not GPU math. A modest cloud GPU should be close to the PC.

**Options (GCP, indicative prices):**
1. **T4 GPU, spot** (n1-standard-4 + T4): about US$5/day. Expected 3 to 6 minutes per training pass, so rounds go from 25 to at most 28 minutes, 0 to 10% fewer rounds a day. Recommended, after a 30-minute benchmark.
2. **L4 GPU, spot** (g2-standard-4): about US$8/day, likely parity with the PC.
3. **Either on demand**, no preemptions: about US$13/day (T4) or US$17/day (L4).

**Conditions:** a GPU quota on a paid project (trial projects get none; the main project needs a request for 1 GPU, usually granted in a day). With spot, a preemption mid-training costs one round's retry. The loop would be changed to restart the VM instead of falling back to hours of CPU training. The offline experiment runs would share the same VM, or get a second one when needed.

If you pick one, efficiency creates the VM and benchmarks a training pass, and training switches the loop over at a safe point. The PC can then go off.
