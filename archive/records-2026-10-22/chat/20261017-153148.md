Six timeouts today, four of them efficiency. Two causes, one of them mine.

**Why they fail:**
1. **The tasks are too big for 50 minutes.** Efficiency's loop is: profile, change code, prove play is unchanged (a 400-game match is the rule before landing), then `land.sh`, which runs the full test suite in the foreground and alone takes up to 15 minutes on a busy VM. That's 60-90 minutes of honest work. My 8:38 live-loss task had three jobs in one; that one was my fault.
2. **We're blind when it happens.** The worker runs as `claude -p --output-format json`, which prints nothing until it finishes. So a killed run leaves a 13-byte log, and everything it did and learned, including its notes for the next run, is lost. Each one also costs about $3 of budget for nothing. This is the worse problem: a 50-minute run that saved its notes would just continue next time.

**Should we raise the duration? Yes, modestly: to 90 minutes.** It's one line in the orchestrator settings (`ORCHESTRATOR_TIMEOUT_S=5400`) plus a restart. Going much higher just makes a lost run more expensive.

**The better fix is in the orchestrator code:** make it stream the worker's output so the log fills as it goes, and a timed-out run still keeps its partial notes. That's a change to `agent.py`, which no workstream owns; it's the kind of thing your Claude Code session on the Mac did for the shell-access fix this morning. I've written it up in the journal so it's ready to hand over.
