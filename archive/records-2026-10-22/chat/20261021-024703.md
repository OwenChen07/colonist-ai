Yes, it does. The engine the search plays through models the snake order exactly: after the first player's settlement and road, the second player places a settlement and road, then places again at once, and only then does the first player get their second placement. So when our bot is second, its first-placement search already sees that its second placement follows immediately and that the opponent's second pick comes after both of ours. The same applies when we are first: the search knows the opponent will take two spots in a row before our second pick.

So the seat gap is not a modelling error. Two other things point the same way:

- **Our own matches show a small first-seat edge.** In the E54 matches, with the same engine on both sides, the first seat won 52.0% to 52.4%, so the engine itself gives seat 1 only 2 to 4 points.
- **Live, the gap is bigger.** Over the last 250 ranked games per account, seat 1 won 63% and seat 2 54%, about 9 points, with an error bar of about 4 points.

That difference between self-play (small gap) and live play (larger gap) is the interesting part. The likeliest explanations are about what humans do with the first pick, not about the rules: strong players going first take the spot the bot values most and then our paired second placement is worse than the net expects, or they use the tempo of the first roll better than our self-play opponents do. Either way it is a question about the opening evaluation against people, which is exactly the opening study live-play has queued, comparing our placements with 2300+ players' in the same positions.
