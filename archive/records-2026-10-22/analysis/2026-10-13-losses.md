# Live losses analysis, 2026-10-13

Read-only analysis. **Constraint:** shell execution was denied in this session,
so nothing below comes from replaying captures or running the net. Every number
is a regex count over `data/human/index.jsonl`, made with Grep. That file has one
row per finished game against a person, with `rolls`, `ended`, `bot_won`, and the
final roads, settlements, cities and dead roads for each side. I took the snapshot
once the per-round rebuild (E3) had stopped writing: 1760 rows, **1677 `ok` games**
(rebuilds append, so later counts will differ).

- "Turns" here means **total dice rolls** in the game, both players together.
- Games against our own bots are already excluded.
- About 98% of rows are ranked; only 23 of all games in `games.jsonl` are casual.
- Intervals are 95% (normal approximation; Wilson bounds at 0% or 100%).
- Q2 and most of Q3 need opponent identities and a replay, which the index
  doesn't hold. Ready-to-run scripts are in `/tmp/analysis/` (see the end of
  this file).

- The bot never resigns: 0 of 722 losses ended by resignation. Opponents resigned
  222 times, by bucket 99 / 84 / 30 / 9.
- **Is the 90+ drop real?** Yes, though it's smaller than the overall-vs-90+ gap
  suggests.
  - 70-89 against 90+: 50.3% vs 42.6%, a gap of 7.6 points (95% CI 1.5 to 13.7
    points, z = 2.4, n = 792 and 373).
  - Games played to 15 only: 48.3% vs 41.2%.
- **But length is not a cause you can steer.** Most of the slope comes from short
  games, which are the ones people resign or get run over in. Once a game reaches
  70 rolls the bot is a coin flip at best. After 90 rolls it is a clear underdog
  and stays at about 41-44%.
- So "the bot falls apart late" is the wrong reading. The right one: against
  people who survive to the midgame, the bot is not better than them, and long
  games are the ones it was losing anyway.

## Q3. How the winner got to 15

Games played to 15 only. Settlements and cities are the final counts on the
board. Building VP = settlements + 2 × cities, so the rest of the winner's 15 (or
more) came from longest road, largest army and VP cards.

| winner's final position | human winners (our losses, n=722) | bot winners (our wins, n=733) |
|---|---|---|
| building VP ≥ 12 (needs ≤ 3 bonus VP) | 160, **22.2%** [19.2, 25.2] | 43, 5.9% [4.2, 7.6] |
| building VP ≥ 11 | 348, **48.2%** [44.6, 51.8] | 163, 22.2% [19.2, 25.2] |
| mean building VP | **10.47** | 9.56 |
| implied VP from LR + LA + VP cards (at least) | ≥ 4.5 | ≥ 5.4 |
| 4 cities | 393, **54.4%** [50.8, 58.1] | 343, 46.8% [43.2, 50.4] |
| 3 cities | 248, 34.3% | 275, 37.5% |
| ≤ 2 cities | 81, 11.2% | 115, 15.7% |
| winner has ≥ 13 roads (road-race proxy) | 353, **48.9%** [45.2, 52.5] | 285, 38.9% [35.4, 42.4] |
| loser has ≥ 13 roads | bot: 190, 26.3% | human: 133, 18.1% |
| bot's dead roads ≥ 4 at the end | 344, **47.6%** [44.0, 51.3] | 114, 15.6% [13.0, 18.2] |

- People beat the bot by **out-building it**. They have more cities, about 0.9
  more building VP, and long road networks. Half of the people who win have 13
  or more roads.
- The bot wins on more bonus VP: army, VP cards, and road when it gets it.
- In losses, the bot is left with 4 or more dead roads three times as often as
  in wins. Those are road branches that reach no building of its own. That
  points to **losing road races**: roads built toward spots it never settled,
  or that the opponent cut off.
- This supports the "races longest road and builds cities" claim. It is about
  people in general, not repeat opponents specifically; Q2 is needed for that.
- **Not computed (needs a replay):**
  - exact longest-road and largest-army holder at the end;
  - VP cards held;
  - who got longest road first, and on which turn.
- `/tmp/analysis/losses_q2_q3.py` computes all of these, plus win rate when the
  bot or the person got longest road first, and whether repeat opponents take
  longest road first more often.

## Q4. Is the opening value error (~0.95) irreducible?

- **Opening** is turn < 20 (`calibrate.PHASES`). Targets are z = ±1 from the
  bot's side.
- **Holdout behind the 0.945 / 0.951 figures:** 194 games. The holdout games
  dated up to the Oct 12 10:00Z snapshot go 105-76, a **58.0% bot win rate**
  (n=181). The current, larger holdout is 136-106, 56.2% (n=242).
- **Best constant predictor:** MSE = 1 − m², where m = 2·0.58 − 1 = 0.16, so
  **≈ 0.974** (0.985 on the current holdout). Its "always win" accuracy is
  **≈ 58%**.
  - This assumes opening positions per game are roughly equal: about 9 per game
    (1755 positions over 194 games).
- **The net (round 19 to 22):** opening MSE 0.945-0.951, accuracy 59.6-60.1%.
  It explains only **about 3% of outcome variance** in the opening (R² ≈ 1 −
  0.945/0.974) and picks the winner 2 points better than always saying "win".
- **Self-play comparison:**
  - The "0.40 self-play" figure is not comparable. It is training validation
    loss against `0.5·z + 0.5·search value` (`train.py --value-mix`, default
    0.5). Its own "always guess 0" baseline is 0.868, not 1.0.
  - Against pure z the self-play MSE would be noticeably higher, so the
    live/self-play gap is overstated.
  - No self-play MSE by phase is logged anywhere: train.log and loop.py only
    report the live per-phase numbers.
- **Trend (different holdouts):** round 1 opening 0.920, late 0.363 (331 games);
  rounds 20-22 opening 0.945-0.951, late 0.504-0.515 (194 games). Not
  comparable, because the holdout was rebuilt, but there is no sign of
  improvement.
- **Verdict: mostly inherent.**
  - To bring the opening MSE down to 0.85, a perfectly calibrated value would
    need to say about 30/70 on a typical position at turn 20. That is not
    credible in a 15-VP game with balanced dice against an unknown person.
  - What predictability there is depends largely on **who the opponent is**
    (rating, repeat history), which the net never sees.
  - Chasing opening MSE through more self-play training is low-value. If
    anything, give the value net opponent-strength context; otherwise judge
    nets on middle- and late-game live MSE.
- To confirm exactly, run `/tmp/analysis/opening_baseline.py
  runs/g1/nets/round_019.npz data/human/holdout/value`. It computes the
  constant baseline per phase on the exact holdout and calls
  `calibrate.calibrate` for the net.

## What this means for reaching rank 1

1. The real gap is the midgame against competent people. From 70 rolls on the
   bot is ≤ 50% (n=1165 games). Long games are where it loses, rather than where
   it collapses.
2. People beat it by out-building it: 4 cities 54% vs 47%, building VP 10.5 vs
   9.6, long roads 49% vs 39%. The bot strands roads in 48% of losses (16% of
   wins). Work on road-race and city-tempo play: the opponent model at the
   opponent's nodes (E2), and human starts.
3. Opening value error (0.945 vs ≈0.974 for a constant) is close to irreducible.
   Stop using it as a target. Watch middle and late live MSE, and fix the
   apples-to-oranges "0.40 self-play" comparison (same pure-z target).
4. Newer nets (bot3, bot4: rounds 16-19) hold up better in long games than the
   `ranked` account's older nets (52% vs 38% at 90+, z ≈ 2.5, confounded). That
   is weak evidence that progress is real, but it is only visible after about
   100+ long games per arm.
5. Before acting on "repeat opponents exploit us", run
   `/tmp/analysis/losses_q2_q3.py` (one process, minutes). The claim is still
   unmeasured; the index has no opponent identity.
